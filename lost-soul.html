    Foreword

    Threads of the Resonance

    Professor Draven Kaelith · Aetherwood Academy · Epoch of Resonant Guardians

    A quiet hush travelled around the room in a wave as Professor Draven quietly stepped to the podium and placed his papers on the lectern.

    The anticipation of each student almost matched my own as they waited for him to speak.

    "Time within the loom is not a linear thread but a vast, interconnected weave, marked by the rhythmic pulse of resonance. These pulses define what we have come to call the Resonance Epochs. More precisely, these are eras of profound change, when balance is tested before being redefined."

    He began without preamble, true to his nature, and addressed his captive audience with the confidence of a man who knew the words he spoke were truth.

    "The standing stones serve as both anchor and testament to these epochs. The rhythms of the resonance are also guided by the lunar cycles, further tying the stones to the undulating pattern. The waxing and waning phases reflect the ebb and flow of light and shadow, along with growth and retreat within the loom. Just as a river may burst its banks when it is filled with rain, so too must the fabric of the loom be treated with care.
    Each action is paired with a reaction. Every emotion, from joy through to sorrow will traverse the resonance and feed its flow. Sometimes this will grow to a point where the web can no longer hold its shape firmly. It reminds us that life can be such a fragile thing. Like the spider spinning her silk to withstand the droplets of the cold morning dew, or the breeze of winter's first kiss, it stands firm until it can no longer hold. Once the tension is beyond its limit, the silk will succumb, inevitably to break and fly on the wind."

    As he spoke, his hands drew patterns in the air to demonstrate his message. Each student watched, completely enthralled in his words, eagerly awaiting the next.

    "In a similar vein, it is a great wonder that the web of the resonance can be replenished. A fracture will heal, just like our own bones, when fed with the right nourishment to continue. It may look a little different once it has reshaped, and it may tell a different story, but who is to say if this was not the intended path all along? If we think back to our spider silk, how will young Anansi ever find its new home if it doesn't allow the breeze to carry it? Our path and destiny may be unknown to us, but the resonance believes in our true self."

    I curiously studied the reaction of the audience as they absorbed this lesson.

    "There is one other thing to consider. When we nourish a wound, we feed it to ensure healthy growth. A lack of the required minerals or nutrients will see the bone grow crooked.
    Again, the resonance has also been observed to behave in this same way. If the wrong source feeds it, then of course that will shape its regrowth. Change is inevitable, a new mould will be created, a new path carved, and a new possibility born. Whilst we as individuals can influence how this will happen, the details of how, what and why remain a secret only told to the loom."

    Draven paused there. I was unable to determine if it was for the gravity of the message, or if he was thinking back to a deeper memory.

    "So how does this intertwine with our own lore here at the academy? That's a question that is impossible to answer in one short seminar, but I am sure you will feel the resonance at work in all you undertake within these halls. Our lore was not written by any single author. It is instead a sequence of records and tales uncovered through the guardians and keepers of time.
    Together, the epochs and cycles provide a history of Aetherwood along with a map of its balance, showing us how the threads have been woven. More importantly, how they must continue to hold. The lunar cycles anchor us to a reality of light, darkness and renewal. May this be your grounding force as you walk through the halls of the Academy."

    The students nudged one another excitedly. A small smile crossed his lips, noticing the willingness for discovery within the room.

    "Let us recap before we close today. The Resonance Epochs and Lunar Cycles are not merely a way to measure time. They are reflections of the loom's infinite designs and mirrors, the balance that governs all things. To understand them is to understand the resonance itself, to see how the threads weave together through light and shadow, love and loss, creation and destruction.
    As we explore tales from our archives, such as The Lost Soul, we glimpse the threads woven in past epochs and cycles. Their lessons are pivotal for guiding us in the present. Even now, in the Epoch of Resonant Guardians, a soft hum can be heard throughout the circle of the standing stones. This is a reminder of all that was sacrificed to preserve the balance, and all that is yet to come."

    As if on cue, the room lit up with bright light as Draven pointed towards the source. Hues of gold, green and red rippled across the walls.

    "Our very own Everflame also reflects the mood of the resonance in some ways. I wouldn't be so bold as to say it is the primary factor, as the Everflame does not burn in this world alone.
    However, we can say with certainty, it is a visible tether to the Loom. Its fire feeds on the Aether itself, flickering with the 'breath' between dimensions. You will learn to read its language over time and may come to understand if it is revelling in this day, a moment that resonated many moons ago, or a tale that is still to unfold. The flame is a curiosity I never grow tired of watching and listening to."
    "The Aether is not a place, but a presence. It is the breath between worlds, the silence between words, the thread between time's unravelling. Where some speak of realms, dimensions, or planes, we speak of the Aether for it is all of these and yet none. The guardians walk its strands unseen, listening for tremors in the resonance, keeping the balance between what was, what is, and what may yet be. To dwell in the Aether is not to travel, but to awaken."

    Part One

    The Watchtower

    I watched him as he stood in the Watchtower, sleepless again, with dawn still far from the horizon. The hours must stretch on as endlessly for him as they do for me.

    Did he know I was there? His third sight made it a real possibility. If he did, he showed no signs, and I refused to dwell on what that meant for me.

    There were times when I longed for him to turn and speak to me. Admit he knew I'd been there all along. Beside him.

    I wanted him to feel me. To know me.

    Reminding myself of my task, I focused once more on his thread and time. The sea of clouds below shimmered beneath the waning moonlight. I could see his shoulders grow tighter as he began recalling memories. I hoped they were good ones.

    Could he see how the moon danced across the ocean on that clear night, when the world had seemed to hold its breath? That night he'd said that everything is always a beautiful dance.

    I'd almost believed him.

    This night, something felt different. The tension in his jaw radiated down past his shoulders into his clenched fists. The silence rang louder than my own heartbeat as it beat its unsteady drum. As he lay on the bed, his eyes drifted shut, just for a moment.

    Did I dare to try? Would he reject me again?

    He didn't move. I couldn't ask him for an invitation, he couldn't hear me. The last time I had ignored my own instincts still haunted me. I still carried a debt to the Loom that would be demanded from me one day.

    I reached out with my mind to his. One silver tendril reaching to find his own, drifting on the breeze, crossing the space between us.

    I found him, standing in a silver fog. The stars above him pulsed like memories. He showed no outward sign of my intrusion. That gave me the confidence to continue. I took him back with me.

    We stood at the edge of a vast abyss, my cloak glowing softly with silver light cast from the moon herself. My back turned to him, yet I knew he felt my presence now.

    "You do not remember me," I spoke to him, without sound in this space we held together. "But you have always known me."

    As I turned to face him, I could see he tried to speak, but no words formed.

    "The Resonance has not abandoned you, Draven Kaelith. I am the echo of your soul that awaits to be reborn."

    I slowly approached him, closing the gap that stood between us and looked into his eyes. They were deep pools that I could lose myself in forever, with my own pearlescence reflected back in them.

    Somehow, I found the strength to continue as I looked into those dark depths to tell him…

    "You are not ready. I know you feel it approaching, the darkness. The time is drawing close, it is almost upon us both."

    The feel of him this close, with our minds entwined was the dance of newly born moth on a gentle summer's night. I was so close to relinquishing my hold and losing myself to the moment entirely.

    Raising my hand, unable to stop, I needed to feel him, I placed it gently upon his chest.

    Again I whispered my lyrical song that only he could hear.

    "With the silence of moonlight and the memory of stars."

    A cool sensation ran through my fingertips as they traced his scar. At that moment Draven awoke with a gasp that cast me away from his mind quicker than I could have foreseen. Torn from his presence, away from his warmth, my essence was shaken to the core.

    The fire had gone out, all light lost, and morning had still not come. He walked towards his desk, to his journal that lay open with pages he'd not written, but my promise inscribed firm and true.

    "With the silence of moonlight and the memory of stars."

    The moon still summoned his gaze as its light danced across the clouds. I heard him whisper a name.

    "Elaris."

    He remembered.

    Would it be enough?

    The Faculty Convenes

    By the time the fire had been rekindled in the Watchtower, with morning finally creeping in along the outer edges of the Aetherwood, a meeting had already been called within the faculty hall.

    I watched from a safe distance, wary of unnerving him, yet drawn to his internal rhythm that pulsed with the same intensity as my own.

    Draven Kaelith, watcher of shadows, keeper of the moonlit forum, master of the Umbraflock and silent guardian of the Loom. He had long observed the Resonance with a steady, unwavering gaze. Within the high stony alcoves of his tower, he listened to the world. Beneath him, the Umbraflock continued their restless circling, calling out to all who would listen. Their eyes saw what no others could, their third sight reaching far beyond the realms of this world.

    I had watched him long enough to know that sometimes, Draven felt like he was a relic of a time long forgotten. He spent most of his days in this tower speaking with the flock. Listening for clues that may never present themselves, studying the archives for any glimmer of information he may have missed. Alone.

    It was obvious to me that the academy was on high alert. The Everflame had done its job, and for that I was thankful.

    They spoke of how the epochs wove their stories, marked by the celestial rhythm of the lunar cycles, each waxing and waning in its influence over the aether. Yet, even in the flowing dance of time, disturbances could ripple through the weave, leaving echoes that demanded attention.

    There was an echo of The Lost Soul that began as a whisper throughout the halls, a haunting summoning to all who would listen. A call that would grow too loud to ignore.

    "What of the Threadsingers, Draven? Have they faltered?" Asked Kaelen.

    "The Threadsingers are a mystery. Whilst their nature is known, little is understood. There is no recorded language, settlement or written history of their time within this Loom. I know them to be real, but I cannot explain how or why."

    I watched him, seated amongst his peers, speaking directly with strength and resolve. How I wished I could give him something tangible to share, so they would believe. I didn't need them, but he did.

    "There are a few records within the archives that touch upon the origins of the Singers. As you all know, this has been my life's work. I want to understand their importance."

    "Whilst it was known they were responsible for the weaving of the threads, the reason why they sung was still a mystery. Most archives are fleeting snippets of remembered songs and folktales found in villages. Perhaps even whispered lullabies to babes as they were rocked to sleep."

    I could see he was internalising. I took that moment to find him on the threads. His weave thrummed gently and welcomed me as I gently asked to entwine.

    The moment I connected, I felt his fear. This was different to our dream walking of the previous night.

    To hold his weave was to know him. He knew the song was changing. He could feel the deep disturbance within the Resonance. It stemmed way beyond the Lost Soul, it was born from the shadows themselves, and he could feel it with every part of his being.

    Usually, our song was a melody of undulating elegance, a honeyed tapestry woven throughout time. Sunset on a summer's eve. Over the last few moon rises, our melody had shifted. A long sorrowful wailing not unlike the banshee's lament. A keening. A knowing. It was clear that something ancient had stirred within the weave. The Lost Soul would soon be upon us again.

    Kaelen's words rang through the room. "And what of the Umbraflock? Have they seen what comes?"

    Draven sighed once more. I felt the weight resting on his shoulders as I remained entwined within his thread.

    He turned to his most trusted ally and looked at her for the longest time before speaking.

    "To understand the signs, I need to know what's happening with your own charge. We can't read the story without all the chapters in place, my friend."

    Kaelen smiled warmly in return. Of all the faculty gathered here this morning, Draven was the one she respected the most. She would never tell him this, but he already knew. That's what he did, know things that should not be known. Hear things that could not be heard.

    Kaelen's hair shimmered as she stood before them, reflecting the sunlight flooding through the faculty chamber. She seemed to suddenly realise it was uncovered and delicately placed her shawl over her head in one quick graceful movement. Instantly, I saw the faculty breathe more easily as their trance was broken.

    She spoke softly to the room, her hands delicately painting her words as she described what she'd seen. How she'd watched the silver threads growing erratic, slowly at first until their chaotic patterns whispered of an imbalance too great to ignore.

    With another flick of her hand, she recreated the flux in the centre of the room so the faculty could see with their own eyes.

    "It's not just the conduit, as you already know Draven, but the stones are awakening too."

    The stones appeared in the weave, one by one. Instantly recognisable as the circle in this valley. They all watched as glyphs began to dance across the surface of the worn stones. Slowly at first, before gaining speed and turning into a crescendo of light and song.

    The song was mesmerising, calling to them all. The Threadsingers needed them to hear a tale that had been dormant for too long. Silver light wound its way around and through the stones with increasing intensity.

    I saw their faces as they heard the warning woven within the song, the urgency and the need. Unease spreading through their souls. Unsure what or who was at risk.

    I noted that not one person was shying away from this calling. That was good. I could not continue if they did not believe.

    I watched Draven rub unconsciously at his chest. I guessed that the scar ached like the old wound it was.

    Draven's voice drifted through the aether.

    "The Loom remembers, even when we forget. When it calls, we must answer."

    Chapter One

    Blood Moon Rising

    The wind howled, chased by its own tortured soul, threading through the graveyard with relentless icy fingers. Tugging at the tattered lace of my wedding dress, now defiled by the dirt and endless driving rain. My gown, this was my magical garment that had been the focus of our women's circle for so long, weeks of stitching by candlelight, with numb fingers. Promises forged with every thread worked, gifts bestowed with each woman's testimony of wisdom and advice, a token of a love eternal, now clung to my stricken body like a burial shroud.

    Beneath my bare feet, the earth was slick with mud, stones scattering under my steps as I staggered onward. My breath came fast, my chest heaving with the effort, yet there was no mist of warmth escaping my mouth in the cold dark night. Instead, a hollow sound of something that should not be.

    I could feel him. His vitality, his presence. Faint as a dying ember in the depths of my being. He was out there, beyond the veil of darkness and rain. They had taken him. Torn him from me in the stillness of our shared grave, the final insult, to be deprived of our promised life and denied the comfort of our eternal sleep, side by side.

    The ache of my loss was unbearable, yet I felt no pain from the jagged rocks that sliced my feet or the brambles that tore at my skin. Something unnatural coursed through me, filling my limbs with strength even as my soul screamed for him.

    I crested the hill, not knowing what drew me on, enticing me despite my shame, horror and awkward jagged movements. There it stood, a lone church, silhouetted against the storm-wracked sky. The glow of candlelight spilled weakly from its windows, its warmth a cruel mockery of the chill that gripped my entire being. This place was familiar, etched in the edges of my fractured memory. Here, beneath those vaulted ceilings, we had pledged our vows. Here, I had whispered his name with every beat of my heart.

    Here, the shadows were coming.

    I stumbled toward the church, my trembling hands pushing open the heavy wooden doors. The scent of wax and incense hit me with a wave of nausea. My gaze swept the nave with a precision I'd never held before. I could see things that should not be.

    A woman kneeled before the prayer candles with tears silently streaming down her face, rosary in hand, mumbling words that would never be heard. She was no longer a part of this time and place. Yet I could see her.

    There were others, some scratching at the walls in terror, others wailing back at the wind. What was this place, and why could I see them?

    There, at the altar, stood the priest. There was something different about him to the ethereal souls that wandered relentlessly around the periphery of my senses. His heart. I could hear it beat with tangible intensity.

    Something deep inside me awoke. A yearning, a desire that I had never felt before. I could hear a low growl echoing through the dais, I realised it was me who had made the sound.

    He turned then at my intrusion, his face pale, his eyes wide with something I could not yet name.

    "Forgive me, Father," I rasped, my voice barely a whisper. "Have you seen him? They took him. I must find him."

    The priest stepped back, his hand trembling as he made the sign of the cross.

    "You! What are you? Why do you mock this sacred ground with your presence?"

    Confusion gripped me. "What am I?" The question reverberated in the hollow cavern of my mind. "I am Neave. I am his bride. I am…"

    Something undeniable began to grow within me. A hunger that I could not ignore. I turned to see what the priest was pointing at.

    I saw it. My reflection, faint and ghostly, in the polished brass of the candelabrum. Streaks of red marred my cheeks, blood, trailing from hollow eyes that no longer held the light of life.

    My lips parted in a silent scream as memories crashed over me. Our wedding night, the dark figure in the shadows, the cold kiss of death.

    I was no longer the bride. I was the cursed.

    The Shadow's Hunger

    The night was alive with whispers. Exactly as I liked it to be, with the wind carrying its secrets to me. What others thought of as howls I heard as gentle caressing promises. They curled through the shadows where I lingered, telling me everyone's most hidden secrets.

    My senses flared as the scent of life stirred around me, the predator within me acutely alert. The rhythmic patter of rain on the ground, along with the soft rustle of leaves, and a faint heartbeat of prey too far to matter, all perfect to hide my own passing.

    Then there was her. Distant. Tracking through the night.

    Neave. My masterpiece. My ruin.

    I had thought the hunger would finally be sated, that her blood would silence the emptiness that gnawed deep within me. So rich and vibrant. Her taste lingered on my tongue, a torment, temptation, and delicious memory all in equal measure.

    She was awake now, no longer the delicate bride cradled in the arms of her lover. She was something more, something greater. A newly woven thread that couldn't be claimed by the Loom. Her essence pulsed a steady enticing rhythm that beat louder with each thrum.

    How could it be that she was not yet mine in entirety?

    I felt her tremor in the threads that bound us. She moved through the night, aimless and unknowing, her will almost bending to the power I had placed within her. Yet there was a faint glimmer of resistance that burned against the call of our hunger. It intrigued me. Infuriated me. Fast becoming the irritation of an itch I could not reach.

    I stepped from the shadows, footfalls silent against the wet earth. My essence at one with the approaching storm, ready and waiting to embrace its chaos.

    The church loomed in the distance, a ghastly beacon of hope against the elements. She was there. I could feel her confusion, her grief, her desperate search for what had been stolen. Her love for him still lingered, a fragile thread that bound her to the mortal life she no longer belonged to.

    Him. Her groom. Anchoring her in death as equally as he did in life, despite being gone. That stirred an irritation in me that I didn't want to acknowledge. Such a human emotion.

    After his death I should have seen his thread passing into the next loom and heard his song. His resounding silence was unnatural. The threads of his being had slipped beyond my reach and into a place I could not follow.

    A puzzle, but not one I cared for. He was inconsequential. Neave was all that mattered.

    The memory of her first breath after the turning was a symphony in my mind. Her eyes had opened, dark and endless. She had awoken something within me, something I thought had been lost to me centuries ago.

    She was perfection, untouched by the scars of mortality, and she was mine to shape. A promise of our eternity.

    How was it that she now stood in defiance? Why did she cling to a love that should have withered in the grave.

    It was almost curious.

    Almost.

    I reached for her through the threads of our bond, a low whisper carried on the storm.

    "Neave," I called, laced with compulsion. "Come to me. Let the pain end. Let me show you what we can become."

    Our binding thread resisted, her will flaring against mine. The defiance sent a new thrill through me, sharp and electric.

    She would come to me, of course. They always did.

    The Fractured Soul

    The voice slithered through the air, soft and insistent, a brush of silk against bare skin yet repulsive in a way I could not describe. It spoke my name.

    "Neave."

    The sound carried longing along with a promise of relief from this constant ache that left me feeling like a hollow version of myself.

    It coiled around my mind, soothing and suffocating all at once.

    I stumbled, grasping at the edge of a pew, nails clawing into the wood to steady myself. My knees buckled, the weight of his call pressing against my chest like a vice. A shadowy thread winding through the fragments of my memories, teasing at moments I could barely grasp, laced with horror and fear.

    "Come to me," it whispered. "Let the pain end. Let me show you what we can become."

    I pressed my hands to my ears to block it out, but it didn't help. The voice resonated from somewhere deeper, threading through the hollow spaces within me. I wanted to surrender. It would be easy to fall into the promise of his words and let them wash away this anguish.

    Suddenly, the image of my groom rose like a spectre in my mind, a flicker of warmth in the darkness. His hand clasping mine, his endless laughter beneath the starlit sky, his eyes seeing right through my soul, the vows we had whispered in this very church. Eternal love. That had been our promise, and I had failed to keep it.

    The darkness pushed at me once more.

    "No," I gasped, my voice trembling. "You can't take that from me. You won't take him."

    I pushed back against the thing inside my mind, against the pull that threatened to unravel me. I could feel his impatience vibrating through me as whatever bound us stretched taut. It burned, a searing pain that coursed through my veins, urging me to yield.

    The priest had already fled, terror etched into his face as he stumbled through the wooden doors into the storm. I was alone, even the spirits I had seen before had abandoned me as the dark shadowy presence loomed ever closer.

    "Leave me!" I screamed into the emptiness of the church. My voice echoed back, hollow and small, with no response.

    The air grew even colder, the flickering candlelight dimming as the shadows seemed to be swallowing it whole. My reflection in the brass candelabrum sneered at me in a grotesque mimicry of my image. The red streaks of blood marking my cheeks the final insult.

    Amidst the dark and growing despair, something deep within me stirred. Whatever I had become, I would not be his puppet, I would not belong to him.

    The storm beyond the mighty oak doors called me like a kindred lost soul as I turned to them.

    The voice whispered again, quieter now, but insistent.

    "You are mine, Neave. You cannot deny it. You are bound to me."

    As I stepped through the doors, the wind continued its howling like the vengeful spirit it was, caressing me in a welcomed embrace. The thread of his call still tugging at me, like rising bile, but I gritted my teeth and pushed forward. Focused on climbing higher toward the ridge.

    "Come to me, Neave," his voice carried on the storm. "There is no escape."

    I whispered into the storm, my voice a vow. "We'll see."

    Defiance

    Her resistance sent ripples through the threads that bound us, each one sharp and searing, a blade drawn across my skin. I had called to her softly at first, a lover's murmur to soothe her pain and draw her closer. Enticing, full of promise of our eternity together.

    I'll admit that at first, her will flaring against mine in such open defiance was something of a thrill. It was quickly souring into frustration the more she fought.

    Annoyingly unexpected.

    The jagged rock of the hillside cut into my back as I leaned against it, using its cover to assess the landscape before me. Its cold, sharp exterior a measure of the storm cascading around me in a fury of rain and wind, and a mirror for my darkening mood. Even the shadows shifted restlessly at my feet, coiling like snakes waiting to strike.

    My call should have been irresistible.

    With Neave, her spark burned brighter with each summons I sent to her.

    That should not have been possible.

    I felt it, her resolute defiance, laced with something more dangerous. There was an intent that unsettled me despite myself.

    She was clever, my Neave, clever and strong.

    That spark drew me to her in the first place. Now it threatened to burn me alive.

    I closed my eyes and reached for her again, weaving my voice into the storm. This time, I did not whisper. I demanded.

    You cannot fight what you are, Neave. You cannot escape me. I can make the pain stop.

    Her answer came swift and sharp, a cutting edge of silence that struck me as true as any blow.

    Her rejection.

    She was choosing to deny me.

    The shadows around me hissed, feeling my irritation. The hunger stirred in my chest, a clawing, gnawing need that could not be ignored.

    I had turned her, crafted her from death itself, and she dared to resist me? To deny what she had now become, the gift I had bestowed upon her?

    The storm screamed louder, as my fury unleashed into its bellowing howl.

    A new calm washed over me.

    I was master of this domain. I controlled the tempest and kept the watchers at bay.

    I almost pitied the humans for their foolish notions of love.

    The memory of her groom imposed on my thoughts. His absence felt like a frayed edge in a carefully woven tapestry, unravelling in ways I could not yet see.

    Neave was all that mattered.

    The wind shifted, carrying her scent toward me, unmistakable in its allure. She was moving, climbing higher into the storm, pushing further from my reach.

    That didn't matter. The bond would guide her to me eventually, whether she willed it or not.

    "Run, my bride," I murmured to the storm. "Run as far as you like. Know you are mine, Neave. You will always be mine."

    The wind howled in reply.

    The Groom's Fate

    The village stayed alive with the sounds of laughter and music as the dancing pushed on into the dawn of the next day. No one wanted to be the first to leave.

    Earlier, beneath the canopy of stars, I had stolen her away from the revelry, my heart full, with eyes only for her. She had laughed as I spun her in my arms, her wedding dress trailing like moonlight on the grass.

    "This is all I'll ever need," I whispered, my voice trembling at the weight of the truth. In that one moment, the world around us dissolved, leaving us at the centre of our own universe. Her warmth banishing the chill of the growing night as she leaned into me.

    Forever did not come.

    The shadows gathered around us as we lay entwined beneath the branches of the trees. A deep unease growing within me. At first, I had thought the play of the wind through the trees could be to blame. The darkness began shifting around us unnaturally. There was a growing oppressive stillness, and a sudden chill that threatened to steal the air from my lungs.

    I rose quietly, instincts stirring my hunter's reflex, as I pulled her up towards me. "Stay behind me," I murmured, as we both stared into the darkness.

    Then it came.

    A figure born of shadow, stepping into the faint glow of the moonlight. Its eyes glowed faintly, embers smouldering in the night. Devoid of warmth. The creature's presence carried an oppressive weight that dug deep into my bones and screamed danger to my very core.

    "Who are you?" I demanded. "What do you want?"

    Its only answer a cruel twist of its lips, teeth too sharp, distorting its face in a sickening substitute for a smile.

    It moved with unsettling grace, closing the distance to where we both stood in an instant. Before I could react, it struck, its hand wrapping around my throat with a force that stole the breath from my lungs.

    Neave. She was my only thought, still behind me. Her gasp of fear a knife wounding my soul.

    That was when I felt the rage. A fire that flared through me even as my body began to betray, my limbs weakening under the creature's iron grip.

    "You will not touch her," I choked out, my voice unrecognisable as my own, raw and defiant. The creature's grip tightened. I fought against it with everything I had.

    The creature's smile faltered.

    "Such a strong soul,"

    it murmured as my eyes fluttered shut beyond my will.

    I felt the sharp sting at my throat, and then the pull, a tide dragging me out to an endless ocean. As my vision dimmed the knowledge of what was happening hit my trembling body in its own tide of sorrow and loss.

    Neave consumed me, as the shadowy haze stole what light was left. Her laughter, her warmth, the vows we had spoken beneath the stars, becoming undone one by one.

    I tumbled further into the depths.

    You will not take her.

    The words roared in my mind as my final strength ebbed away. In the moments before my breath stilled, something shifted.

    I felt it, the thread snapping loose. A piece of myself breaking free and hurtling into the unknown.

    A shooting star, falling faster than imaginable, plummeting into the darkness of this night.

    The creature released me, letting my limp body fall to the ground. I heard him murmur "such passion" as it licked the remnants of my life from its lips. "Such devotion. You will serve me well."

    Even as the creature turned its attention to Neave, there was a part of me that lingered.

    Not as I once was, no longer trapped within my vessel of flesh and bones, and yet defying the shadows as I twisted and slipped beyond the creature's grasp.

    Even as the darkness took me, I whispered through the veil, willing myself into the space between life and death.

    She will not fall, not if I can hold the thread.

    Like a singer with no voice, I waited.
